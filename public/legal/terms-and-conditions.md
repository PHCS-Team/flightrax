## 1. What FlightraX Is

FlightraX is a system for planning, authorising, and recording flight training
operations. It is used to file flight plans and weight and balance sheets, to
request and approve flights, to publish the daily aircraft schedule and NOTAMs,
and to keep flight logs.

FlightraX is a record-keeping and coordination tool. **It does not authorise
flight.** It does not replace the judgement and authority of the pilot in
command, the supervision of a flight instructor, the instructions of air
traffic control, or any requirement of the Civil Aviation Authority of the
Philippines (CAAP) or of the flight school's own operations manual. Where this
system and an applicable regulation or instruction differ, the regulation or
instruction governs.

## 7. Flight Plans, Weight and Balance, and Approval

Flight plans and weight and balance sheets that you file are operational
documents. You must not enter information you know to be inaccurate, and you
must not file a flight plan for an aircraft or a crew you are not authorised to
use.

A flight request must be reviewed before the flight proceeds. A reviewer may
approve it or reject it with a reason. Approval in FlightraX records that the
request was authorised within the system; it does not by itself make a flight
lawful or safe.

## 8. Scheduling, Delay, and Automatic Cancellation

Aircraft are a shared resource. A flight left open on the board blocks the
aircraft for everyone booked after it.

For that reason, a scheduled flight that has not commenced is treated as
follows:

- **60 minutes** past its planned departure time, the participants are notified
  that the flight is running late.
- **90 minutes** past its planned departure time, the flight is cancelled
  automatically and the aircraft is released for other bookings.

An automatic cancellation is recorded as such. If you are delayed, commence or
cancel the flight yourself rather than leaving it open.

## 9. Records of Who Did What

FlightraX records, for each flight, who submitted it, who approved or rejected
it and when, who commenced and terminated it and when, and who cancelled it and
when. Flight logs, schedules, NOTAMs, and account decisions are recorded in the
same way.

These records exist so that mistakes can be traced and corrected and so that
the flight school can meet its record-keeping obligations. They form part of the
flight school's operational and training records, and they are retained.

## 10. What Is Visible to the Public

Most of FlightraX is visible only to signed-in users whose role permits it. Two
things are not, and you should know about them before you register.

**The flight monitor board.** FlightraX publishes a live flight board, intended
for a screen in the lobby, which anyone on the internet can reach without
signing in. For each of that day's flights it shows the student's name and the
instructor's name — shortened on screen to a first initial and surname, for
example "K. Bautista" — along with the aircraft registration, destination, and
departure and arrival times. The data feed behind the board carries the full,
unshortened names. If you fly, your name and your movements for that day are
public.

**Profile and aircraft photographs.** These are stored in public storage, so
anyone holding the direct link to an image can open it without signing in.
Choose a profile photograph with that in mind.

Your ID document, licence images, and certificate images are **not** public.
They are kept in private storage and opened only through links that expire.
